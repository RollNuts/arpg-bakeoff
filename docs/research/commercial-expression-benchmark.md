# Commercial Expression Benchmark

Status: market-facing expression research for the solo fantasy ARPG direction.
Date: 2026-06-30.

Use references as market evidence only. Do not copy their characters, costumes,
UI, worlds, palettes, trailers, logos, music, weapons, bosses, or named systems.

## Core Finding

Successful commercial action RPGs do not sell a weapon first. They sell a role,
a place, a threat, and the promise that the player's mastery will change the
state of that place.

For this project, the store-facing promise is:

- become a lone swordfighter
- enter an ancient sealed kingdom
- survive readable, weighty melee combat
- open shortcuts and return to altars
- defeat five guardians
- bring light back to dead sanctuaries

Weapon art matters only when it clarifies the player's combat decision and the
world fantasy.

## Benchmarks

| Game | Market Signal | What The Store/Trailer Sells | Expression Principle To Absorb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No Rest for the Wicked | Premium ARPG attention and strong material craft. | Painterly dark world, heavy manual melee, crafted environments. | The screen must show craft and hit weight, not just darkness. |
| Kena: Bridge of Spirits | Small-team premium third-person fantasy benchmark. | Beautiful dangerous spaces, readable hero, polished effects. | A bright fantasy world can still feel dangerous when enemy reads are clear. |
| Death's Door | Compact action-adventure benchmark. | Small clear hero, melancholy world, dense boss-focused adventure. | Short games can sell through identity, density, and clean combat. |
| Tunic | Compact exploration and shortcut curiosity. | Mysterious world, gates, hidden paths, readable exploration loops. | Exploration value can fit in dense spaces instead of huge maps. |
| Dark Souls III / Elden Ring | Boss and stamina-combat reference. | Ancient spaces, readable danger, shortcut/checkpoint tension, victory relief. | Boss tells and retry rhythm are more important than raw difficulty. |
| V Rising | Dark fantasy readability reference. | Clear player/enemy separation and strong lighting in dark scenes. | Darkness needs strong value separation and readable action shapes. |
| Diablo IV / Path of Exile 2 | Mainstream ARPG production expectation. | Heavy impacts, thick UI/audio, boss spectacle, deep progression image. | Weight and spectacle matter even when this project stays smaller. |

## Trailer And Screenshot Patterns

- First 3-5 seconds show the promise, not a tutorial prompt.
- Strong trailers alternate world, traversal, combat, boss, reward, and identity.
- HUD is hidden or reduced in early store-facing shots, then shown when it proves
  combat readability.
- A hero needs a value/color accent that survives stone floors and fog.
- Hit impact usually uses a bright contact core, colored outer shape, hit stop,
  knockback, and debris/body reaction.
- Bosses sell by screen control: large silhouette, clear windup, and a small
  readable player.
- A screenshot should expose the world rule: sealed gates, altar checkpoints,
  sacred light returning, or a guardian blocking progress.

## Audio Patterns

The sound identity should be describable in one line:

> sealed stone kingdom, steel impact, low bells, distant choir, and boss-scale
> pressure.

Avoid generic cinematic fantasy. The mix should let sword hits, guard impacts,
boss windups, altar sounds, and UI confirmations read clearly.

## Implications For This Project

The previous generated concepts often skewed toward rugged armor plus oversized
weapons. That can support enemy, boss, or impact references, but it is not enough
to define the hero.

The current product needs:

- one clear solo protagonist identity
- third-person silhouette and rear readability
- four weapon families with distinct timing and stance
- five area identities that are visible in screenshots
- bosses that sell the Steam page before system depth does
- UI and audio that look/sound like a product from the first accepted capture
