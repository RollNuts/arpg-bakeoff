# Commercial Expression Benchmark

Status: market-facing expression research for the cooperative fantasy action
direction.
Date: 2026-06-30.

Use references as market evidence only. Do not copy their characters, costumes,
UI, worlds, palettes, trailers, logos, music, weapons, maps, or named systems.

## Core Finding

Successful cooperative action games do not sell a weapon first. They sell a
shared problem, clear roles, readable pressure, and a moment where players have
to talk.

For this project, the store-facing promise is:

- become a small shrine expedition team
- escort a dangerous holy reliquary
- swap tools to change roles
- keep sacred fire alive
- solve blockers while enemies attack
- protect a vulnerable ritual
- seal the temple before the relic breaks

Weapon art matters only when it clarifies role, urgency, and the cooperative
screen read.

## Trailer And Screenshot Patterns

- First 3-5 seconds show the shared problem, not a tutorial prompt.
- Strong trailers alternate tool choice, objective pressure, enemy pressure,
  environmental solution, ritual climax, and title.
- HUD is hidden or reduced early, then shown when it proves objective tension.
- Each player needs a tool silhouette or value/color accent that survives stone
  floors and fog.
- Hit impact usually uses a bright contact core, colored outer shape, hit stop,
  knockback, and debris/body reaction.
- Cooperative screenshots should show at least two simultaneous problems.
- A screenshot should expose the world rule: reliquary, sacred fire, tool stand,
  blocker, guardian, or ritual.

## Audio Patterns

The sound identity should be describable in one line:

> sacred fire, unstable reliquary magic, heavy shield/axe impact, clear bell
> calls, and ritual pressure.

Avoid generic cinematic fantasy. The mix should let sword hits, shield blocks,
torch/fire, bell calls, reliquary warnings, and ritual progress read clearly.

## Implications For This Project

The previous generated concepts often skewed toward rugged solo combat. That can
support enemy, guardian, or impact references, but it is not enough to define
the game.

The current product needs:

- two readable pilgrims at gameplay scale
- carried tools that read instantly
- a reliquary that is the screen's central object
- sacred fire that creates visible safety
- enemies that pressure roles and objectives
- UI/audio that make danger and role state obvious
- screenshots where the game cannot be mistaken for generic combat
